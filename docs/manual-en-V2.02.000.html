1. What the app does

Snippet Book stores text you use often — commands, notes, IDs and passwords — and copies it with a single click (or a tap on a phone).

Text you used to type every time can now be pasted in one step. Everything you register is stored on your own computer or phone, protected by encryption. It is never sent over the internet.

Useful when you…
  • type the same server login command again and again
  • often enter email addresses or passwords
  • reuse the same standard phrases
Language
  • The app's screens are available in Japanese and English (Version 2.02.000 and later). At first the app follows your browser's language (Japanese for a Japanese browser, English otherwise).
  • To switch, use 日本語 | English at the top right of the lock screen, or Language at the bottom of the key settings (chapter 12).
  • Button names in this guide are those of the English screens.

2. First-time setup

When you open the app for the first time, the Get started screen appears.

  1. Press Create new. (If you have a backup from the previous version, press Start from a backup — see chapter 13.)
  2. Your recovery key is shown. It has 25 characters, like K7QM3-XTP9A-…. Save it with Print or Copy (for your password manager).
  3. To confirm, type the last 5 characters of the recovery key and press I have saved it.
  4. When asked "Register a passkey on this device?", press OK and register with your fingerprint, face, etc. (or your computer's password). If asked where to save it, iCloud Keychain is recommended on iPhone and Mac.
The most important warning

The recovery key is like a spare house key. Once you close that screen, it can never be shown again. If you lose both your passkeys and the recovery key, nobody — not even this app — can open your data. Print it or save it in a password manager, and keep it somewhere other than your backup files. Do not show it to anyone.

3. Unlocking — passkey and recovery key

Usually: unlock with a passkey

Press 🔑 Unlock with passkey on the lock screen and confirm with your fingerprint, face, etc. (the same way you unlock your device). Your data opens as you left it. There is nothing to type.

When a passkey is not available: use the recovery key

Type your saved recovery key in the Open with recovery key box and press Open with recovery key. The - separators are optional, and lowercase is fine. The digit 0 and the letter O, and the digit 1 and the letters I and L, are read as the same.

When you step away, press 🔒 Lock at the top right to lock immediately.

After 5 minutes without activity the app locks itself. You can choose 1, 5, 15 or 30 minutes, or Never, in the key settings (chapter 12). Time spent in another tab or app also counts as inactivity. While the recovery key screen is shown you get extra time, but the app still locks after 10 minutes. If a confirmation dialog ("Continue?" etc.) stays open past the time limit, the app locks as soon as it is closed and the operation is cancelled (this is not treated as pressing "Cancel"; you will see "Locked because there was no activity for too long").

When the app locks (manually or automatically), it clears everything that was on the screen: cards, edit fields, the search text, the password-sharing screen and any recovery key you were typing. If you were exporting, sending or importing, that operation is cancelled — unlock and do it again. The same applies to registering a passkey or replacing keys: the keys stay as they were.

When you press Lock, the screen is cleared first, before saving finishes. If something is still being saved, "🔒 Locking…" is shown for a few seconds. If saving does not finish within 5 seconds, your edits are kept encrypted with your key, and the next time you unlock you are asked whether to restore them. Locking always finishes within about 7 seconds. If your edits could neither be saved nor kept, the unlock screen says "Your last changes before locking could not be protected". After unlocking, check whether your last changes are there.

Devices without passkey support

Older devices or browsers may not be able to register a passkey (the app says it is not supported). On such devices, unlock with the recovery key each time. Your data is protected just as well.

4. Basic use — copying

When the Copy button at the top is selected, you are in copy mode. This is the normal mode.

  1. Click (or tap) the text of the card you want to copy.
  2. A message confirms the copy, and the card is briefly stamped Copied.
  3. Paste it where you need it as usual (⌘+V on a Mac, Ctrl+V on Windows).
Copying with the keyboard (computers)

Use the number at the left of each card: ⌘+1 to ⌘+9, and ⌘+0 for the 10th card, copy that card directly. (On Windows use the Ctrl key.)

5. Adding and editing cards

Press Edit at the top to switch to edit mode.

  • Add: create a new card with the Add button.
  • Change: just retype the text on the card. There is no Save button — changes are saved automatically as you type.
  • Reorder: use ↑ ↓ at the right of a card. Putting the ones you use most at the top is handy.
  • Delete: ✕ deletes the card.

When you are done, press Copy to return to copy mode.

6. Storing IDs and passwords

For login details, where an ID and a password belong together, use a special card.

  1. In edit mode, press Add ID/password.
  2. Enter the ID / user name and the Password.

Back in copy mode, the ID and the password each have their own Copy button. The password is masked (●●●) so nobody can read it over your shoulder. Press 👁 to show it for 3 seconds.

With the keyboard (computers)

On ID/password cards, ⌘+number copies the password and ⌘+Shift+number copies the ID.

7. Organizing cards into sets

You can keep separate notebooks for different purposes, for example "Servers" and "Email". The sets appear as tabs at the top; click one to switch.

In edit mode, + (Add a set) appears to the right of the tabs. Click the current tab again to rename it. × deletes the current set.

Keyboard shortcuts (⌘+number) work on the set currently shown.

8. Backups — protecting your data

Stored data can occasionally be lost (especially on phones). With a backup, you can restore it.

How to make a backup

  1. Press 💾 Back up & lock at the top right.
  2. In Chrome and Edge on a computer, a dialog to choose the location appears; choose a folder and save. On iPhone and iPad, "Ready to save" appears: press Choose where to save and pick "Save to Files" in the share sheet to choose any folder, such as iCloud Drive. Browsers without such a dialog (Safari on Mac, Firefox, etc.) save to the downloads folder.
  3. The file name is "data name-date-time-obk-tag.enc.json" (for example snippet-book-20261103-1405-obk-7K3QX9MZ2D.enc.json). The last 10 characters are a tag made from the SHA-256 of the file, using a key that only your recovery key or passkey can open. Do not rename the file.
  4. When "Backup complete" appears, press Lock. You return to the lock screen.

A banner at the top recommending a backup is also your cue to make one. It also appears after you register a passkey or replace the recovery key.

Two kinds of export
  • Back up & lock: an "overwrite backup" for your own safekeeping and for syncing devices. It opens with a passkey or the recovery key.
  • Export all, encrypted (at the bottom): a full backup. Choose "Cancel" for a file that opens with your passkey or recovery key, or "OK" for a file that opens with a password just for that file (for giving to someone). That password must be 12 characters or more; longer is safer.
Where to keep backups

Encrypted backups protect their contents, but do not keep them in the same place as your recovery key (with both, the backup can be opened). Files made with "Export as plain text" can be read by anyone; do not put them in shared cloud folders, for example.

9. Using it like an app (Home Screen, Dock, install)

Opening it from an icon makes it work like an app, and on phones your data is less likely to be cleared.

DeviceHow
iPhone / iPad (Safari)Share button ⬆︎ at the bottom → "Add to Home Screen". If you added it before, remove it and add it again to get the new icon.
Mac (Safari)Menu "File" → "Add to Dock".
Mac / Windows (Chrome)The "Install" icon at the right of the address bar, or menu → "Cast, save, and share" → "Install".
Android (Chrome)Menu → "Add to Home screen" or "Install app".

Opened from the icon, the data and passkeys are the same as in the browser.

Back up phones often

Phones lose stored data more easily. After any important change, make a backup with "Back up & lock".

View only

On devices where you do not want to leave data (a shared computer, for example), choose "📄 Just read a file (nothing is saved)" on the start screen. You can open a backup file to view and copy only. It opens with a passkey or the recovery key. Nothing remains after you close it.

10. Using it on another device / keeping devices in sync

There is no automatic sync, but you can sync by hand with backups. The safest way is to treat one computer as the "main" device and copy from it to the others.

Starting on a new device

  1. On the main device, press "Back up & lock" to make a file.
  2. Send the file to the new device (AirDrop, email attachment, cloud, etc.).
  3. On the new device, open https://snippet.eiwa-up.jp/ and choose the file with Start from a backup.
  4. Open it with a passkey (synced passkeys, e.g. via iCloud, work as they are) or the recovery key.
  5. When asked "Register a passkey on this device?", register one.

The recovery key is the same on every device (a new one is not created).

Bringing an existing device up to date

  1. On the main device, press "Back up & lock", make a file and send it.
  2. On the lock screen of the other device, choose the file with Open from an overwrite backup.
  3. Unlock with a passkey or the recovery key. The contents are replaced.
Beware of overwriting

Importing deletes the previous data on that device and replaces it with the chosen contents. To avoid confusion about which is newer, we recommend deciding to "always edit on this device only".

11. Giving a password to someone safely

Use Send this set, encrypted at the bottom.

  1. The set currently shown is encrypted and saved as a file.
  2. A password just for that file is made automatically and shown. It has 20 characters, like AB3CD-EF4GH-JK5MN-PQ6RS, and is different every time.
  3. Save it with Copy. The "-" characters are part of the password. It is safest to copy and paste it rather than retype it.
Always do this

Send the file and the password by different routes. For example, the file by email and the password by phone, SMS or CP2P. Putting both in the same email defeats the purpose of encryption.

The recipient chooses the file with Import and enters the password to add its contents to their own notebook.

If you sent files with Version 2.00.002 or earlier

The automatic passwords of those versions were short and could be guessed, given time, by someone who obtained the file. If you sent important passwords or API keys, sending them again is not enough (old files may still exist and could be read).

  1. Change the passwords themselves at the services they belong to. Revoke and recreate API keys.
  2. Then send the new passwords with "Send this set, encrypted" in the current version.

13. Moving from Version 1

The previous version (Version 1) was opened with a master password at https://cp2p.eiwa-up.jp/snippet-book/. Since 4 November 2026, that address automatically takes you to the new address. If you have a backup made with Version 1:

  1. Open https://snippet.eiwa-up.jp/ and choose the backup with Start from a backup.
  2. Enter the master password when asked (this is the last time you use it).
  3. Your recovery key is shown. Save it as described in chapter 2 and register a passkey.
  4. Make a new-format backup with 💾 Back up & lock. You can throw away the old-format backup.

Data stored in the browser under the old address remains in that browser. If you have a device you have not moved yet, please contact us.

15. Important rules for staying safe

  • Always save your recovery key. If you lose both your passkeys and the recovery key, your data cannot be recovered.
  • Keep the recovery key and backup files in different places. With both, a backup can be opened.
  • Lock your device (passcode, fingerprint, face). Passkeys open the same way you unlock your device.
  • Protect important data with backups — especially on phones, and often.
  • Use the same device, browser and address. A different place means different data.
  • Be careful where you keep plain-text exports. Anyone can read them.
  • When giving a file to someone, send the file and the password by different routes.

The app runs only on your device. There is no mechanism that sends your entries to the internet. The font is served together with the app, and nothing is loaded from other sites.

What it cannot protect against (to be honest)
  • If the server delivering the app is taken over and the program is replaced with a malicious one, data opened during that time could be read. The comparison on GitHub (chapter 16) is for detecting such a replacement, not for preventing the reading itself.
  • It also cannot protect you if the device itself is compromised (malware, remote control, etc.), or if someone uses it while it is unlocked.
  • When locking, data is cleared from the screen at once, but it may stay in the browser's memory a little longer.

16. Checking that the app is genuine (SHA-256)

You can check that the app file has not been modified using its "hash value" (SHA-256). If it matches the value published on the official site and GitHub, the app is genuine.

VersionSHA-256 (snippet-book.html)
Version 2.02.00094b9f522820c557dd83a199b57bcf68922af768df8af2d925bcec20bab86df89

On a Mac (Terminal):

curl -s https://snippet.eiwa-up.jp/ | shasum -a 256

On Windows (PowerShell):

Invoke-WebRequest https://snippet.eiwa-up.jp/ -OutFile snippet-book.html; Get-FileHash snippet-book.html -Algorithm SHA256
